\section{Introduction}

Financial statement fraud imposes significant costs on investors, creditors, and capital
markets. Detecting fraud ex ante—before regulatory enforcement actions—remains a central
challenge in forensic accounting. This report develops a linear probability model to predict
whether a firm-year is subject to an SEC Accounting and Auditing Enforcement Release
(\textsc{aaer}), our proxy for fraudulent financial reporting.

We estimate the following model on a panel of firm-years from 2003 to 2014:

\begin{equation}
  \text{FRAUD}_{it} = \alpha + \theta_1\,\text{MFLAG}_{it}
                             + \theta_2\,\text{FFLAG}_{it}
                             + \beta_1\,\text{ACCRUAL}_{it}
                             + \beta_2\,\text{ASSET\_GROWTH}_{it}
                             + \beta_3\,\text{LEV}_{it}
                             + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}

\noindent
where FRAUD is a binary indicator equal to one if firm $i$ in year $t$ appears in the
\textsc{aaer} database. We include two established fraud-detection composites
(Beneish's M-Score flag and Dechow et al.'s F-Score flag) alongside three additional
financial statement variables that capture distinct fraud incentives.

We estimate the pooled model over the full 2003--2014 period (Part~I) and validate it
year-by-year to assess the temporal consistency of each predictor (Part~II).

% ============================================================
\section{Variable Definitions and Theoretical Predictions}

\subsection{Dependent Variable}

\textbf{FRAUD} is an indicator variable equal to one if the firm-year appears in
the SEC's \textsc{aaer} database compiled by Dechow et al. (2011), and zero otherwise.
The \textsc{aaer} dataset covers enforcement actions from 1982 to 2014 and is matched
to Compustat using \texttt{gvkey} and fiscal year.

\subsection{Required Predictors}

\textbf{MFLAG} (Beneish M-Score Flag) equals one if the firm's M-Score exceeds
the threshold of $-1.78$, the cutoff identified by Beneish (1999) as indicative
of earnings manipulation. The M-Score is a weighted combination of eight financial
ratios capturing changes in receivables, gross margins, asset quality, sales growth,
depreciation, SG\&A, accruals, and leverage:

\begin{align*}
  \text{MSCORE} = &-4.84 + 0.920\,\text{DSR} + 0.528\,\text{GMI} + 0.404\,\text{AQI} \\
                  &+ 0.892\,\text{SGI} + 0.115\,\text{DEPI} - 0.172\,\text{SGAI} \\
                  &+ 4.679\,\text{ACCRUALS} - 0.327\,\text{LEVI}
\end{align*}

\noindent\textit{Predicted sign: positive.} Firms with distorted financials across
multiple dimensions are more likely to be committing fraud.

\vspace{0.5em}

\textbf{FFLAG} (Dechow F-Score Flag) equals one if the firm's F-Score exceeds 1.0,
where the F-Score rescales the predicted fraud probability from a logistic regression
by the unconditional fraud rate ($0.37\%$). The F-Score model uses seven components:
\texttt{RSST} (accruals), \texttt{CH\_REC}, \texttt{CH\_INV}, \texttt{SOFT},
\texttt{CH\_CS}, \texttt{CH\_ROA}, and \texttt{ISSUE}:

\begin{align*}
  \hat{y} = &-7.893 + 0.790\,\text{RSST} + 2.518\,\text{CH\_REC} + 1.191\,\text{CH\_INV} \\
             &+ 1.979\,\text{SOFT} + 0.171\,\text{CH\_CS} - 0.932\,\text{CH\_ROA} + 1.029\,\text{ISSUE}
\end{align*}

\noindent
$\text{FSCORE} = \frac{e^{\hat{y}}/(1+e^{\hat{y}})}{0.0037}$; $\quad$
$\text{FFLAG} = \mathbf{1}[\text{FSCORE} > 1]$

\noindent\textit{Predicted sign: positive.} Firms with F-Scores above 1.0 have a
predicted fraud probability exceeding the base rate, indicating heightened risk.

\subsection{Additional Predictors}

\textbf{ACCRUAL} $= (IB - OANCF)/AT$ measures total accruals scaled by total assets.
High accruals represent a wedge between cash flows and reported earnings—a classic
signal of earnings management (Sloan, 1996). Fraudulent firms often sustain high
accruals to maintain the appearance of profitability.
\textit{Predicted sign: positive.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\textbf{ASSET\_GROWTH} $= (AT_t - AT_{t-1})/AT_{t-1}$ measures the year-over-year
growth in total assets. Rapid asset expansion may signal acquisition-driven fraud,
channel-stuffing, or capitalization of expenses that should be recognized immediately.
It also proxies for the pressure on management to justify elevated valuations with
continued headline growth.
\textit{Predicted sign: positive.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\textbf{LEV} $= (LCT + DLTT)/AT$ measures total financial leverage. Firms with
high debt loads face covenant pressure and may misreport to avoid technical defaults
or to maintain access to credit markets.
\textit{Predicted sign: positive.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent\textit{Note on excluded variables.} We initially included \textbf{ROA}
and \textbf{SALES\_GROWTH}. ROA $(= IB/AT)$ was dropped due to near-perfect
collinearity with ACCRUAL (Pearson $r = 0.905$), since both share $IB$ in the
numerator and $AT$ in the denominator. SALES\_GROWTH was dropped because extreme
outliers (standard deviation of 61, maximum of 11{,}880) rendered the variable
uninformative in regression (coefficient $\approx 0$, $t = -0.23$).

\section{Data and Sample}

Financial statement data come from Compustat (1990--2019). Fraud data come from the
SEC \textsc{aaer} database covering enforcement releases from 1982 to 2014. We restrict
the regression sample to fiscal years 2003--2014 to match the period of reliable
\textsc{aaer} coverage used in Dechow et al.\ (2011).

We require: total assets $(AT)$, sales $(SALE)$, and net property, plant and equipment
$(PPENT)$ all positive; and no missing values for \texttt{cogs}, \texttt{dp},
\texttt{rect}, \texttt{act}, \texttt{lct}, \texttt{ib}, and \texttt{oancf}.
Construction of F-Score additionally requires two years of lagged receivables.

\textbf{FFLAG} is the strongest and most statistically significant predictor
($\hat{\beta} = 0.0073$, $t = 8.31$). Firms flagged by the F-Score are approximately
0.73 percentage points more likely to be fraudulent, which represents an economically
large effect given the unconditional fraud rate of $0.70\%$—effectively a near-doubling
of fraud probability.

\textbf{MFLAG} carries a positive sign consistent with theory but is statistically
insignificant ($t = 0.22$). This is consistent with Dechow et al.\ (2011), who show
that the F-Score has substantially greater discriminatory power than the M-Score when
evaluated on out-of-sample data. The M-Score was designed for a narrower set of
manipulators and is less well-calibrated to SEC enforcement actions.

\textbf{ACCRUAL} is negative but insignificant. While accruals are theoretically
motivated as a fraud signal, this variable's information content is largely subsumed
by FFLAG, which incorporates accruals (as \texttt{RSST}) as one of its seven
components.

\textbf{ASSET\_GROWTH} is positive and marginally insignificant ($p = 0.107$), which
is directionally consistent with the prediction that aggressive expansion signals
managerial incentives to manipulate.

\textbf{LEV} is negative and insignificant. The unexpected sign may reflect that
highly leveraged firms face greater creditor scrutiny, reducing the ability to commit
fraud—a mechanism not captured by our simple prediction.

The low $R^2$ ($0.0018$) is expected in models predicting rare binary events and
is consistent with values reported in the academic literature on fraud prediction.

\subsubsection*{Consistency of FFLAG}

FFLAG is significant at the $5\%$ level or better in 8 of 12 years (2003--2006,
2011, 2012, 2014) and marginally significant in 2007 ($p = 0.068$). Its coefficient
is consistently positive across all twelve years, ranging from $0.0013$ in 2008 to
$0.0210$ in 2003. This consistency is strong validation of the F-Score as a fraud
predictor across time.

The notable exceptions are 2008--2010, which correspond to the peak and immediate
aftermath of the financial crisis. We attribute the loss of significance to two
mechanisms. First, the fraud rate dropped sharply (from $1.57\%$ in 2003 to
$0.44\%$ by 2008), reducing statistical power. Second, \textsc{aaer} enforcement
lags actual fraud by several years, so the 2008--2010 firm-years may contain fewer
completed enforcement actions in the database, compressing the variation in the
dependent variable.

\subsubsection*{Consistency of MFLAG}

MFLAG is never statistically significant at conventional levels in any individual
year. Its sign alternates between positive and negative, suggesting it carries
little reliable incremental information about fraud beyond what is captured by
FFLAG and the other predictors. This is consistent with the pooled result.

\subsubsection*{Consistency of Additional Variables}

ASSET\_GROWTH achieves significance in 2005 ($t = 2.04$) and 2014 ($t = 2.25$),
consistent with the prediction that aggressive expansion signals manipulation,
particularly in years with elevated capital market activity.

LEV is negative and approaches significance in 2005 ($t = -1.99$), suggesting that
during the mid-2000s credit boom, the disciplining effect of creditor monitoring
may have suppressed fraud at highly leveraged firms. In later years, the sign
reverses as the economic environment changed—though LEV is never significant after
2007.

ACCRUAL does not achieve significance in any individual year, consistent with
its lack of incremental explanatory power in the pooled model.

\section{Conclusion}

We develop a linear probability model to predict accounting fraud using Compustat
financial data merged with SEC \textsc{aaer} enforcement records. Our key findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{FFLAG is the most powerful and consistent predictor.} Firms flagged
    by the Dechow F-Score are approximately twice as likely to commit fraud as
    unflagged firms, and this result holds in 8 of 12 individual years.
  \item \textbf{MFLAG adds no statistically significant incremental information.}
    The M-Score, while conceptually sound, appears subsumed by the more
    comprehensive F-Score in this setting.
  \item \textbf{Asset growth shows episodic predictive power,} particularly in
    years of active capital market expansion, consistent with the theory that
    rapid growth creates pressure to misreport.
  \item \textbf{The financial crisis (2008--2010) disrupts prediction,} likely
    due to reduced enforcement activity rather than an actual decline in fraud.
\end{enumerate}

These findings have practical implications for auditors and regulators seeking
to allocate scrutiny efficiently across large populations of public firms.

The following SAS code was used to construct all variables and produce the results
reported above. All steps are commented to document methodological choices.

\lstset{style=sas}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Assignment 2: Full SAS Program}]
/*=================================================================
  ACCT 270 - Group Assignment 2: Predicting Accounting Fraud
  Model: Fraud = a + b1*MFLAG + b2*FFLAG + b3*ACCRUAL
                   + b4*ASSET_GROWTH + b5*LEV + e
  Sample: 2003-2014
=================================================================*/

libname mydata "s:\sasdata";
libname home   "d:\users\mcbai\desktop\programs";
options ps=max ls=max nocenter;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1: CONSTRUCT M-SCORE (Beneish 1999)
  MSCORE = -4.84 + 0.92*DSR + 0.528*GMI + 0.404*AQI + 0.892*SGI
           + 0.115*DEPI - 0.172*SGAI + 4.679*ACCRUALS - 0.327*LEVI
  MFLAG = 1 if MSCORE > -1.78
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

/* Examine AAER dataset to confirm structure and coverage */
proc contents data=mydata.aaer1982to2014; run;
proc freq data=mydata.aaer1982to2014; tables fyear; run;

/* Pull and clean Compustat: require positive AT, SALE, PPENT;
   no missing core variables needed for M-score and F-score     */
data variables;
    set mydata.compustat1990to2019;
    if AT > 0 and SALE > 0 and PPENT > 0;
    if nmiss(cogs, dp, rect, act, lct, ib, oancf) = 0;

    /* Set missing optional items to zero */
    if dltt = . then dltt = 0;
    if xsga = . then xsga = 0;
    if am   = . then am   = 0;
    if invt = . then invt = 0;
    if che  = . then che  = 0;
    if dltis= . then dltis= 0;
    if sstk = . then sstk = 0;

    /* M-Score raw components (current-year values) */
    DS      = RECT / SALE;                    /* Days sales receivables */
    GM      = 1 - (COGS / SALE);             /* Gross margin           */
    AQ      = 1 - ((PPENT + ACT) / AT);      /* Asset quality          */
    SG      = SALE;                           /* Sales level (for SGI)  */
    DEP     = (DP - AM) / (DP - AM + PPENT); /* Depreciation rate      */
    SGA     = XSGA / SALE;                   /* SG&A to sales          */
    ACCRUAL = (IB - OANCF) / AT;             /* Total accruals (Z3)    */
    LEV     = (LCT + DLTT) / AT;             /* Leverage (Z5)          */

    /* ROA computed but excluded from regression:
       collinear with ACCRUAL (r = 0.905, both use IB/AT) */
    ROA = IB / AT;
run;

/* Merge current year to prior year for M-score ratios
   and additional lag-dependent variables                */
proc sql;
    create table addlagyear as
    select a.*,
           b.DS as DS_m1, b.GM as GM_m1, b.AQ as AQ_m1,
           b.SG as SG_m1, b.DEP as DEP_m1, b.SGA as SGA_m1,
           b.ACCRUAL as ACCRUAL_m1, b.LEV as LEV_m1,
           b.AT as AT_m1, b.IB as IB_m1,
           b.RECT as RECT_m1, b.INVT as INVT_m1, b.SALE as SALE_m1
    from variables a
    inner join variables b
        on a.gvkey = b.gvkey and a.fyear - 1 = b.fyear;
quit;

/* Compute M-Score indices, MSCORE, MFLAG, and growth variables */
data Mscore;
    set addlagyear;
    /* Index ratios: set to 1 if denominator is zero (neutral) */
    if DS_m1  > 0 then DSR  = DS  / DS_m1;  else DSR  = 1;
    if GM     > 0 then GMI  = GM_m1 / GM;   else GMI  = 1;
    if AQ_m1  > 0 then AQI  = AQ  / AQ_m1; else AQI  = 1;
    if SG_m1  > 0 then SGI  = SG  / SG_m1; else SGI  = 1;
    if DEP    > 0 then DEPI = DEP_m1 / DEP; else DEPI = 1;
    if SGA_m1 > 0 then SGAI = SGA / SGA_m1; else SGAI = 1;
    if LEV_m1 > 0 then LEVI = LEV / LEV_m1; else LEVI = 1;

    MSCORE = -4.84 + 0.92*DSR + 0.528*GMI + 0.404*AQI + 0.892*SGI
             + 0.115*DEPI - 0.172*SGAI + 4.679*ACCRUAL - 0.327*LEVI;
    if MSCORE > -1.78 then MFLAG = 1; else MFLAG = 0;

    /* Asset growth and sales growth using lagged values */
    if AT_m1   > 0 then ASSET_GROWTH = (AT   - AT_m1)   / AT_m1;
    if SALE_m1 > 0 then SALES_GROWTH = (SALE - SALE_m1) / SALE_m1;
run;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 2: CONSTRUCT F-SCORE (Dechow et al. 2011)
  Requires 2-year lagged receivables for CH_CS component
  FFLAG = 1 if FSCORE > 1.0
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

/* Get 2-year lagged receivables from raw Compustat (not cleaned)
   to maximize sample coverage at the 2-year horizon            */
proc sql;
    create table addlagyear2 as
    select a.*, b.rect as rect_m2
    from Mscore a
    inner join mydata.compustat1990to2019 b
        on a.gvkey = b.gvkey and a.fyear - 2 = b.fyear;
quit;

/* Compute 7 F-Score components, predicted log-odds, probability,
   F-Score, and FFLAG                                           */
data Fscore;
    set addlagyear2;
    if nmiss(rect,rect_m1,rect_m2,ib,ib_m1,invt,invt_m1,
             che,at,at_m1) = 0;

    RSST   = ACCRUAL;                          /* Accruals          */
    CH_REC = (RECT - RECT_m1) / AT;           /* Change receivables */
    CH_INV = (INVT - INVT_m1) / AT;           /* Change inventory   */
    SOFT   = (AT - PPENT - CHE) / AT;         /* Soft assets        */
    CH_ROA = (IB/AT) - (IB_m1/AT_m1);        /* Change ROA         */

    /* Change in cash sales ratio */
    if SALE_m1 - (RECT_m1 - RECT_m2) > 0
        then CH_CS = ((SALE - (RECT - RECT_m1)) /
                      (SALE_m1 - (RECT_m1 - RECT_m2))) - 1;
        else CH_CS = 1;

    if DLTIS + SSTK > 0 then ISSUE = 1; else ISSUE = 0;

    /* F-Score logit equation */
    PredictedValue = -7.893 + 0.790*RSST + 2.518*CH_REC
                     + 1.191*CH_INV + 1.979*SOFT
                     + 0.171*CH_CS - 0.932*CH_ROA + 1.029*ISSUE;

    /* Cap at 709 to prevent exp() overflow */
    if PredictedValue > 709 then PredictedValue = 709;

    Probability = exp(PredictedValue) / (1 + exp(PredictedValue));
    Fscore      = Probability / 0.0037; /* Scale by unconditional rate */
    if FSCORE > 1 then FFLAG = 1; else FFLAG = 0;
run;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 3: MERGE WITH AAER, CODE FRAUD, RESTRICT TO 2003-2014
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

/* Left join: preserve all firm-years; AAER match => fraud */
proc sql;
    create table addAAER as
    select a.*, b.P_aaer
    from FSCORE a
    left join mydata.aaer1982to2014 b
        on a.gvkey = b.gvkey and a.fyear = b.fyear;
quit;

data Fraud;
    set addAAER;
    if 2003 <= fyear <= 2014;            /* Analysis window          */
    if nmiss(mflag, fflag) = 0;          /* Require both flags       */
    if nmiss(accrual, asset_growth, lev) = 0; /* Additional vars    */
    if not missing(P_aaer) then FRAUD = 1; else FRAUD = 0;
run;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 4: DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

proc means data=fraud n mean std p25 p50 p75 maxdec=3;
    var fraud mflag fflag accrual asset_growth lev;
quit;

proc freq data=fraud; tables fyear; run;

proc means data=fraud mean maxdec=4;
    var fraud; class fyear;
run;

proc corr data=fraud;
    var mflag fflag accrual asset_growth lev;
quit;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 5: PART I - POOLED REGRESSION (2003-2014)
  Model: FRAUD = a + MFLAG + FFLAG + ACCRUAL + ASSET_GROWTH + LEV
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

proc reg data=fraud;
    model fraud = mflag fflag accrual asset_growth lev;
    title 'Part I: Pooled Fraud Regression (2003-2014)';
quit;

/*-----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 6: PART II - YEAR-BY-YEAR REGRESSIONS
  Same model estimated separately for each fiscal year.
  BY statement in PROC REG loops over sorted fyear groups.
-----------------------------------------------------------------*/

proc sort data=fraud; by fyear; run;

proc reg data=fraud;
    by fyear;
    model fraud = mflag fflag accrual asset_growth lev;
    title 'Part II: Year-by-Year Fraud Regressions';
quit;
